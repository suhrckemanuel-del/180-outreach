What we are trying to do

Win two or three paid student-consulting projects for 180 Degrees Consulting Delft & Rotterdam by mid-January 2027, by offering nonprofits and social enterprises one concrete automation we can build with them.

We do that with cold email, written from what an organisation publishes about its own work. The target is about 1,000 emails by 22 December, the day the sector closes for the holidays.

2 of 5 · The system

How the 180 system works

Nine steps from a public register to a booked call. Everything except the research and the writing runs as plain code, with no AI cost.

  1. Registers become a candidate list. Free public sources (the ANBI charity register, Social Enterprise NL, the Aedes housing map, Rotterdam's published subsidies) are turned into one list with a website, city and segment per organisation.
  2. The enrich gate, with no AI cost. A small program reads the organisation's own website and scores twelve signals: a real intake form, a funder, a staff size, a portal, an admin vacancy. Only organisations above the threshold go on to research.
  3. A research card. One page per organisation: the workflow we could automate, the funding, the people, and a verdict with reasons and a source link for every fact.
  4. An email from a template. Two alternating arms: one leads with track record, one with two concrete automation ideas from their own site. The writer fills the template, it does not invent the shape.
  5. Lint and a second checker. A linter enforces the rules: one question, one link, no unverifiable claims, every claim about us tagged against the ledger, every fact about them tagged with its page.
  6. A Gmail draft. The draft is created through the Gmail interface, with the footer and the opt-out line added by code so they can never drift.
  7. Manuel approves. The first thirty first-emails, and every tier-1 email, wait for an explicit approval command. No model can approve.
  8. Sending with a ramp. A plain script sends only what is ready, inside working hours, with a random gap between sends, a daily cap that grows from 20 to 65, and an automatic pause if bounces cross three percent.
  9. Follow-ups, replies and calls. One follow-up after a week, in the same thread; replies are classified and drafted for Manuel to answer; a call planner prints the number and the opening line for the calls he makes himself.

Why the gate is code and not AI

Asking a model about every candidate costs roughly seventeen thousand tokens each, and most candidates are clearly wrong for us. Twelve cheap signals remove the obvious misses for zero tokens, so the model only ever looks at organisations that already look plausible.

Why one card per organisation, written once

The card is the single source of truth for the email, the follow-up and the call. Writing it once and reusing it stops a later session from inventing a different fact about the same organisation, and it makes every sentence traceable to a page.

Why no model can send, approve or answer a reply

Sending is the one irreversible step. Splitting drafting from sending means a mistake costs a review, not a burned domain. Replies get a drafted answer for a person to send, because a wrong answer to a real organisation is worse than a slow one.

Why the daily cap grows slowly

A new inbox on a shared domain has no sending reputation. Twenty, then thirty-five, then fifty, then sixty-five a day builds one without tripping spam filters, and the bounce pause protects the domain if a list turns out to be stale.

3 of 5 · Two kits

What is shared with the career kit, and what is separate

This kit was built from Manuel's personal career-outreach kit. The ideas and several code patterns are shared; the data, the inbox and the decisions are not.

Shared

The same design: templates with fixed shapes, a linter that enforces one ask and one link, a footer and opt-out line added by code, a registry that stops two people writing to the same organisation, and the same Gmail plumbing.

Separate

Its own sending inbox, its own ledger of approved claims, its own suppression list, its own pipeline, state folder, reports and daily schedule. Nothing from the career kit's contacts, messages or notes lives in this kit, and this explainer contains no career data.

The one thing not shared yet

There is no shared do-not-contact list. If an organisation asked the career kit to stop writing, this kit does not know that automatically. Until the two lists are joined, that check is manual.

Why we built it this way

Reusing the proven parts saved weeks, and copying the data would have been both pointless and a privacy risk. One kit per sender organisation also keeps the legal footer, the claims and the reply handling honest: each inbox only speaks for itself.

4 of 5 · Gaps and risks

What is not finished, and what could go wrong

Legal basis for cold email open

Dutch rules (Telecommunicatiewet 11.7) treat unsolicited commercial email carefully, even to organisations. The file docs/lawful-basis.md sets out our reading and the open questions; the legal team has not answered them yet. Volume should not rise before it does.

The footer has no postal address open

The email identifies the sender, the organisation and where the address was found, and offers an opt-out, but the sender's postal address is not configured. Dutch practice expects a real identification block; this is a one-line fix in the tenant settings, waiting on a decision.

English emails to Dutch organisations known

Every email is in English (or German for Germany, Austria and Switzerland), because that is what the team can check. Some Dutch nonprofits will find that odd. The alternative, Dutch templates, needs a native reviewer before it can go out.

Sensitive sectors known

Youth care, healthcare, debt assistance and refugee work appear in the list. A careless sentence about someone's clients is a real harm, not a marketing miss, so those cards get a second read and the case-file wording is still being reviewed.

Sixteen decisions are still blank blocked

Segments, the childcare register, the January volume, a second inbox, the legal questions and the project wording all wait on Manuel. A blank answer means the session skips that work rather than guessing, which is why the lead engine has not run at volume yet.

The Gmail login can expire known

The Google app is still in "Testing" mode, where the login token can expire after seven days. The daily run checks the login first and stops loudly instead of failing silently; publishing the app removes the trap.

Why we built it this way

Every item above is written down where the team can see it, instead of being discovered during a bad week. The kit prefers refusing to send over sending something it cannot justify: a blank decision stops the work, and a failed login stops the run.

5 of 5 · Vision

Where this goes, and the next ten days

The vision is a small, boring engine the branch can run without a specialist: a public list goes in, a ranked shortlist and approved emails come out, and every week the numbers say what to do next.

What that looks like when it works

  • A new member of the branch can produce a week of outreach in an afternoon.
  • No email goes out that a person has not approved or a rule has not checked.
  • The report answers "how are we doing" in one page, without opening a spreadsheet.
  • Two or three signed projects per semester, from a funnel the team can actually see.
